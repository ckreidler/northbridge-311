Water and sewer
Utilities guidance for the fictional City of Northbridge Water Department.

Q: When is a water issue an emergency?
A: Treat these as emergencies and call 311 immediately (after hours the line routes to the on-call utilities desk): uncontrolled water in the street, a sewer backup into a home, or a hydrant that is sheared off. If you smell natural gas, call 911, not 311.

Q: How do I report a street leak or sinkhole?
A: Give the nearest address, whether water is bubbling or pooling, and if the pavement is settling. 311 opens an NB-###### utilities request. Crews may barricade the area before a full repair.

Q: Who owns the water line to my house?
A: The city owns the main in the street and the service line up to the curb stop. The property owner owns the line from the curb stop into the building, plus indoor plumbing. 311 can send an inspector to help locate the curb stop but cannot repair private plumbing.

Q: How do I ask about a water bill?
A: Have the account number from the bill or the service address. Billing questions are taken during 311 weekday hours. The assistant can explain typical charge types (water, sewer, stormwater) but cannot waive a balance. Payment plans are handled by Utilities Customer Accounts at Civic Plaza.

Q: What should I do if my sewer is backing up?
A: If only your property is affected, a private plumber should check the building lateral first. If neighbors on the same block also have backups, call 311 so crews can inspect the city main. Do not use running water until the backup is cleared.

Q: Is the tap water safe to drink?
A: Northbridge Water publishes an annual quality report. Unless the city issues a boil-water notice, tap water is treated and tested to meet drinking water standards. 311 will announce boil-water areas if a main break requires one.
